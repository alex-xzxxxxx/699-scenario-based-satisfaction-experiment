## D-001 — Term compressed from 14 weeks to 13
**Date:** 2026-10-29
**Decision:** The proposal's 14-week plan (Table 14) is executed in the 13 active
weeks of the CISC 699 term (26 Oct 2026 – 14 Feb 2027, two scheduled breaks
excluded). The pilot and the Week 6 presentation now share a week; everything
after it shifts one week earlier.
**Reason:** The term has 13 instructional weeks, not 14.
**Affects:** M1–M8 week numbers; the two-week re-judgment gap (preserved:
human evaluation Week 9, re-judgment Week 11 = 14 calendar days).
**Status:** Closed. Decided by the researcher; no external acknowledgement
sought. Nothing in the proposal's design was dropped — only the calendar
changed — so the compression is reported as a schedule change in the final
report, not as a scope change.

## D-002 — Application stack: Node/Express + plain HTML/JS
**Date:** 2026-11-08
**Decision:** The task-tracking application (V0–V3) is a small Node/Express
server with a plain HTML/JavaScript front end and server-side sessions. No
front-end framework.
**Reason:** The proposal (§3.12) fixes the application's *behaviour* — five use
cases, user accounts, access control — but not its stack. A small codebase was
chosen because: V0 must be generated by the implementation model from the
requirements document in one pass on a free API tier, so fewer files means
fewer failed or partial generations; three copies must be made and each seeded
defect confirmed by hand (T16, T17), which scales with code size; and access
control needs real server-side sessions, which a front-end-only app cannot
provide honestly.
**Alternatives considered:** Angular + Express API (3–4× the generated code,
higher free-tier token cost, no benefit to the research question);
Flask + Jinja (equivalent, but mixes Python into the application and blurs the
harness/application separation).
**Affects:** T05 requirements document; T06 V0 generation; T16 defect seeding.
**Cannot change after:** V0 is tagged (M2, Week 3), because V1–V3 are copies.

## D-003 — Both test baselines are browser-level Playwright tests
**Date:** 2026-11-08
**Decision:** Suite A (written by the implementation model alongside V0) and
suite B (hand-written, about 20 tests) are both browser-level Playwright tests
run through pytest. The requirements document states this so that suite A comes
back in that form.
**Reason:** EQ4 compares defect detection between the two suites and the
scenario evaluation. If suite A were unit tests and suite B browser tests, the
two baselines would differ in kind and the comparison would not be clean. Making
both browser-level also keeps the baselines independent of the application's
language.
**Affects:** T05 requirements document; T07 suite B; T08 and T24 execution; EQ4.

## D-004 — Success thresholds, fixed before any data exists
**Date:** 2026-11-08
**Decision:** The proposal (§4.8) left the thresholds marked as proposed. They
are now set, by me, and frozen. They are recorded here before V0 exists, before
any scenario is written, and before any judgment is collected. The dated commit
and the `prereg-w01` tag are the evidence of that ordering.

**EQ2 — agreement.** Three figures are reported together, each with a 95%
interval:

| Symbol | What it is |
|--------|------------|
| κ_LH | LLM judge vs my first human judgments |
| κ_HH | my first judgments vs my delayed re-judgments (T27) — my own repeatability |
| — | the Landis & Koch (1977) band, for orientation only |

Pre-specified rule: **agreement is acceptable if the 95% interval for κ_LH
reaches κ_HH**, that is, the judge is not demonstrably less consistent with me
than I am with myself. Whether κ_LH ≥ 0.61 is reported alongside, with its
interval, as a conventional reference point — not as a pass or fail.

**Reason:** κ_HH is the honest ceiling. An LLM judge cannot sensibly be asked to
agree with me more than I agree with myself, and the proposal (§4.6) already
names this as the realistic target. A fixed cut at 0.61 would also be unsound
here: with about 25 human-judged runs the interval on kappa is roughly ±0.2 to
±0.3, so a point estimate landing either side of 0.61 would not support a
conclusion. The confusion matrix is reported next to kappa in every case, as
§4.6 requires.

**Affects:** T26, T27, T28; EQ2; the acceptance criterion in C05.

## D-005 — Critical-failure gate: a flag beside the scores, never replacing them
**Date:** 2026-11-08
**Decision, two parts.**

*(a) Scoring a version.* All four schemes are computed and reported side by
side for every version: binary, linear 4-3-2-1, geometric 8-4-2-1, and the
critical gate as a **separate boolean flag**. The gate does not overwrite the
weighted totals. The release-style reading is gate first; the analysis keeps
every number visible.

*(b) Judging the judge.* Critical-severity false acceptances are reported as a
**count with a Wilson interval**, not as a zero-tolerance pass or fail. If any
occurs it is reported prominently as a finding whatever the other results say.

To make (b) mean anything, the human subset (T26, about 25 runs) must contain
**at least 8 critical-severity runs, at least 4 of them known-failing** from the
defect register. This is a pre-specified sampling constraint on T26.

**Reason:** (a) EQ1 asks whether severity weighting changes conclusions versus
binary scoring. If the gate overwrote the scores, that comparison could not be
observed at all — the gate would answer EQ1 by erasing it. (b) "No critical
false acceptance" sounds strong but is close to empty at this sample size:
0 out of 4 critical runs has an upper bound near 0.6, which supports no claim
about how often the judge does this. Fixing a floor of 8 critical runs at least
makes the interval reportable and states its width honestly.

**Affects:** T20 scoring engine; T26 sampling; T28, T29; EQ1, EQ2, EQ3.

## D-006 — Thresholds set by a single researcher, declared as a limitation
**Date:** 2026-11-08
**Decision:** D-004 and D-005 were set by me alone, without external review.
This is recorded as a limitation in the final report's threats section, with the
mitigation stated plainly: the thresholds were fixed and committed before any
data existed, and the commit date and tag can be checked.
**Reason:** Setting one's own criteria is defensible when they are
pre-specified and unchangeable afterwards; it is not defensible if they are
chosen once the results are visible. The dated commit is what separates the two,
so the report states it rather than leaving the reader to wonder.
**Affects:** Report threats to validity; C06.

## D-007 — Model selection: Claude implements, Gemini judges
**Date:** 2026-11-08
**Decision:** Two models, as the proposal specifies (§4.12: "one judge model").

| Role | Model | Family | Calls |
|------|-------|--------|-------|
| Implementation | Claude (Anthropic) | Anthropic | a few: V0 and suite A, Week 3 |
| Judge | Gemini (Google), free tier | Google | about 540, Weeks 7–8 |
| Human reference | Xuan Zhao | — | about 25 blind, plus about 10 re-judged |

**Reason:** The families differ, which is the constraint §2.x imposes to avoid
self-enhancement bias — no model judges software it wrote. The judge carries
almost all of the call volume, so it is the role that needs an API with a
workable daily quota; the implementation model is called only a handful of
times and can be driven through a chat interface. A second judge model is
WP11, optional and after M6, so it is out of scope.

**To record before Week 7** (T02, still open as O5): the exact Gemini model id,
its version string as returned by the API, and the free-tier requests-per-day
limit read from the console with the date it was read.

**Reproducibility note:** the implementation model is reached through a chat
interface, so the serving model version is not exposed as precisely as an API
call would give. LLM generation is not bit-reproducible in any case. What is
reproducible, and what is therefore retained, is the exact prompt, the exact
raw output, and the tagged V0. Stated plainly in the report rather than
claimed away.

**Selection criterion for the judge — mechanical reliability only.** Before
Week 7, run a ten-call smoke test: valid JSON every time, rubric fields and
permitted values respected, stable under repetition, acceptable latency and
timeout rate. **The judge is never selected, tuned, or swapped on the basis of
how well it agrees with my own judgments.** Doing so would choose a judge that
flatters the human reference and would void EQ2. If the judge has to be
replaced after the smoke test, the reason is recorded here and reported.

**Affects:** T02, T18, T25; EQ2, EQ3; Week 7–8 pacing.

## D-008 — The requirements document mandates stable test selectors
**Date:** 2026-11-08
**Decision:** `app/REQUIREMENTS.md` §3 requires every interactive element to
carry a named `data-testid` attribute, and every message to appear in one
container with a known id.
**Reason:** The proposal (§4.12) names scenario scripting as the riskiest part
of the project and schedules it early for that reason. Unstable or invented
selectors would put Weeks 4–5 at risk and would add nothing to any research
question.
**Threat, to be reported:** this makes the generated application more testable
than an AI-generated application would typically be, which may flatter both
test suites and the scenario runner. Mitigation: the constraint is identical
for V0 and V1–V3, so it favours no version; and it says nothing about what will
be tested, so it does not leak the holdout.
**Affects:** T05, T06, T13, T15; EQ4, EQ5; report threats section.

## D-009 — Evidence is a compact trace, not raw HTML
**Date:** 2026-11-08
**Decision:** `evidence.py` writes a condensed, judge-ready trace per run:
the scenario steps, the action taken at each step, the visible text that
changed, any message shown, the final state, and the run outcome. Raw page
source is not written into the evidence passed to the judge.
**Reason:** Judge cost is dominated by evidence length. One raw HTML page runs
to tens of thousands of tokens; across 540 calls that is millions of tokens and
would not fit a free tier. Beyond cost, a long dump dilutes the judge's
attention, and the human evaluator reads the same evidence — 25 runs of raw
HTML is not readable, and an unreadable reference is a worse reference.
**Both judges see exactly the same evidence.** If they saw different material,
EQ2 would be comparing two different tasks.
**Affects:** T14, T18, T19, T25, T26; EQ2, EQ5; judge token cost.

## D-010 — Judge model is gemini-3.6-flash, requested by that id
**Date:** 2026-11-08
**Decision:** The judge is Gemini `gemini-3.6-flash`. It is requested by that
exact id, never by `gemini-3.5-flash` and never by an alias such as
`gemini-flash-latest`.

**Reason — the requested id does not pin the model.** The T02 smoke test
(`records/W01/judge-smoke-test.json`) requested `gemini-3.5-flash` ten times
and the API answered with `modelVersion: gemini-3.6-flash` every time, with
nothing in the response signalling the substitution. Requesting
`gemini-3.6-flash` returns `gemini-3.6-flash`. Same model served either way,
but only one of the two ids tells the truth in the record, and the freeze at
M5 depends on being able to say which model produced each judgment.

Aliases ending in `-latest` are excluded for the same reason, more strongly:
they are documented to move. The 540 judgments run across Weeks 7 and 8 with a
two-week break between them, so an alias could point at one model before the
break and another after it, splitting the dataset between two judges with no
record of where the split fell.

**Mechanical result:** fifteen successful calls across the two smoke runs, all
returning valid JSON with the identical verdict at temperature 0; median
latency 2.4 s. The failures observed were quota, not model behaviour.

**Threat, to be reported:** a served model that differs from the requested one,
silently, is itself a finding for a study about when an LLM judge can be
trusted. It belongs in the threats section, not only in this log.

**Affects:** T18, T25; EQ2, EQ3, EQ5.

## D-011 — The judge run is resumable, quota-aware, and version-checked
**Date:** 2026-11-08
**Decision:** `judge.py` must:
1. cache every response, keyed by run id and judgment index, and skip completed
   work on restart;
2. record `modelVersion` on **every one** of the 540 calls, not once at the
   start, and halt if it ever changes mid-run;
3. pace requests, retry a 429 with backoff, and on a 429 that does not clear,
   stop cleanly and report how far it reached rather than burning retries.

**Reason:** the smoke test hit the free-tier quota after roughly twenty calls
in one evening, and backing off 8 s then 16 s did not clear it, so the limit is
not only a per-minute burst. 540 calls will therefore span several days. A
single long-running process cannot be assumed to survive that, and a crash
halfway through must not mean re-judging from zero. The proposal anticipated
this: judge runs are to be "spread over several days rather than done in one
sitting" (§4.12). Point 2 follows from D-010.

**Still open (O5):** the free-tier RPM and RPD for `gemini-3.6-flash`, to be
read at ai.dev/rate-limit. 540 calls at 1000 RPD is one day; at 50 RPD it is
eleven, which would move judging to start before the 21 Dec – 3 Jan break
rather than after it.

**Affects:** T18, T25; Week 7–8 scheduling.

## D-012 — Requirements v1.1: ambiguities closed before V0 is generated
**Date:** 2026-11-16
**Decision:** `app/REQUIREMENTS.md` goes to v1.1 before V0 is generated. Seven
new requirements (R24–R30), eleven clarifications to existing ones, and three
new constraints in section 1. R1–R23 keep their numbers, so the use-case
mapping holds; the new ones are appended.

**Reason:** a review of v1.0 found sixteen points where two competent
implementations would reasonably differ. Since the document states that nothing
outside it is part of the specification, each gap would have produced
disagreement between suite A, suite B and the judge for reasons unrelated to
the application's quality — noise in exactly the measurements EQ2 and EQ4 are
made of.

Three mattered more than the rest:
- **Storage caching.** v1.0 required `npm run reset` before each test but never
  said the server must re-read from disk. An implementation that cached
  `data/db.json` in memory would have made reset a no-op, and every test after
  the first would have run on contaminated state — silently, with the suite
  still passing. v1.1 requires a disk read per request.
- **Toggle authorisation.** `/tasks/:id/toggle` is a write and no requirement
  covered it; only the general R21 applied. Now R24.
- **Distinguishable 404s.** R17 demanded a 404 for another user's task but said
  nothing about ids that do not exist, so sequential ids could be enumerated by
  comparing responses. Now R25.

**Left unresolved on purpose:** R2 reveals whether a username exists, while R5
conceals it. That is a genuine tension in the specification, not an oversight,
and it is reported in the final report rather than patched.

**Still unspecified on purpose:** how thorough suite A should be (see D-003 and
the T05 notes). Fixing its form keeps it comparable with suite B; fixing its
coverage would answer EQ4 by instruction.

**Ordering note.** Several of these ambiguities are good candidates for seeded
defects. They are not chosen now. Defects are selected and registered at T16 in
Weeks 5–6, *after* the scenario library is frozen at T12, so that scenarios are
derived from the use cases and never written to target a defect that is already
known.

**Affects:** T05, T06, T07, T10, T11, T16; EQ2, EQ4, EQ5.
**Cannot change after:** V0 is tagged at M2.

## D-013 — EQ4 is re-scoped: what each method can find, not how much
**Date:** 2026-11-17
**Decision:** EQ4 stops measuring a detection delta between the scenario
method and a conventional suite, and starts measuring which *kinds* of finding
each method is capable of producing. The pre-registered thresholds set in
D-005 are not touched, and no other evaluation question changes.

**Reason:** the premise EQ4 was built on does not hold for this artefact, and
my own baseline is what demonstrates that.

EQ4 was drawn from the reported finding that machine-generated test suites
systematically under-cover requirements. The question only has force while the
conventional baseline is weak: if suite A is thin, anything the scenario
method finds that suite A misses is evidence for the scenario method. Suite A,
written by hand in Week 2 against the frozen specification, came out at 230
assertions referencing all thirty requirements, and passes on V0. Suite B is
25 tests written independently and also passes. The baseline is not thin.

### Why the premise failed here

This is a property of the setup, not a correction to the literature. Three
reasons, all mine:

- **The specification is unusually complete.** D-012 closed sixteen
  ambiguities before V0 was generated. The thin suites reported in the
  literature are written against thin or implicit specifications.
- **The requirements are numbered.** Suite A was written against an explicit
  checklist. Covering a list is a different task from inferring what is worth
  testing.
- **The system is small.** Thirty requirements over five use cases can be
  covered exhaustively. The cited work uses codebases where that is not
  feasible.

So the premise is scope-dependent and this artefact sits outside the scope
where it holds. That goes in the report as a limitation of the design.

### The new measure

Every finding produced by any method is classified:

| Class | What it is | Which methods can produce it |
|---|---|---|
| A | Behaviour contradicts an explicit requirement | suites, scenarios, judge |
| B | The requirement is silent or ambiguous; two reasonable implementations differ | scenarios and judge; a suite only by accident |
| C | Behaviour meets the letter of the requirement but not the scenario's expected behaviour | scenarios and judge only |

A test suite can only produce class A by construction. An assertion *is* a
decided expectation, so it cannot register that the expectation was never
decided. A scenario carries its expected behaviour in prose and the judge
reads that against the evidence, so B and C are reachable.

This stays falsifiable. If the 47 scenarios across four versions yield no
class B or C finding that the suites missed, the re-scoped EQ4 answers *no*,
and that is reported as the result.

### The evidence that cuts against the re-scoping

The two class-B findings in hand — the undefined post-submit redirect and the
"always present" message container — were produced by **suite B, not by the
scenario method**. They surfaced because two independent readers of the same
specification implemented it differently. Writing a second independent suite
is itself a way to reach class B.

The re-scoped question therefore has to be sharper than "can scenarios reach
class B at all". It is whether the scenario route reaches it more
systematically, or at lower cost, than writing a second independent suite.
That comparison is part of what EQ4 now reports, and it may well come out
against the scenario method.

### Why this is not a post-hoc rescue

- **Pre-registration binds decision rules, not premises.** What D-005 froze
  were thresholds and the rules for reading results. A premise the data
  contradicts is not something a threshold protects.
- **No experimental data exists yet.** No scenario has run against V1, V2 or
  V3. The only EQ4-relevant data in hand comes from V0 and the two suites, and
  that data is what forced the change. There is no outcome to fit this to.
- **The alternative is worse.** Reporting a coverage delta against a premise
  my own baseline contradicts would misrepresent the study.
- **Silence is what makes a mid-study change illegitimate.** This one is
  dated here, carried in the Week 4 and Week 5 records, and stated on the
  slide.

**Affects:** T16, T26, T30–T33; EQ4 only. EQ1, EQ2, EQ3 and EQ5 are unchanged,
as are the D-005 thresholds and the D-009 evidence format.
**Cannot change after:** the first judge run of the main experiment (T25).

## D-014 — The evidence records response headers, from an allowlist
**Date:** 2026-11-17
**Decision:** The collector copies an allowlist of response headers into
`network.jsonl`, starting with `cache-control`, and `network.jsonl` is now
scrubbed like every other file in a bundle. This amends T14, which W04 records
as complete.

**Reason:** SES-13 asks whether a protected page is still reachable with the
browser Back button after signing out, and R28 answers it with
`Cache-Control: no-store`. Behaviour alone cannot settle it. A page that was
never stored and a page that was stored but happens not to be redrawn look
identical from outside the browser, and which one the application produced is
exactly the difference R28 is about. Without the header, SES-13 would be
judged against evidence that could not contain its answer, in all four
versions — a false negative built into the instrument rather than observed in
the system.

### Why an allowlist and not every header

Copying every header would cost more than it returns. D-009 keeps a bundle
small enough that the whole judge run fits a free tier, and most headers —
`date`, `etag`, content lengths, the server's own name — say nothing about
whether a scenario was satisfied while giving a judge more text to wander
through. The list is a constant in `harness/runner/runner.py` and takes one
line to extend when a later requirement needs another header.

### The scrubbing that came with it

`network.jsonl` was the one file in a bundle written without scrubbing. That
was harmless while an entry held only a status and a relative path. It stops
being harmless the moment the server's own output is copied through, so the
entries now pass through the same `_scrub` as the trace, the console log and
the final state. This is a correction to T14 rather than a new feature, and it
is recorded here rather than folded into the change above.

### Cost, measured

Re-running the whole library after the change: `trace.json` is unchanged at a
median of 2,929 bytes, because headers go to `network.jsonl`; that file has a
median of 900 bytes and a maximum of 2,569; the complete evidence for 47 runs
is 294 KB. The D-009 budget is not touched.

### Timing

Made before T12 freezes the library and before any V1, V2 or V3 run exists, so
no judged data was produced under the old format and none has to be discarded.
Changing the evidence format after the main run had started would have been a
different and much worse decision.

**Affects:** T14, T15, T24, T25; SES-13; EQ2 and EQ3 through it.
**Cannot change after:** the first judge run of the main experiment (T25),
with D-013.
