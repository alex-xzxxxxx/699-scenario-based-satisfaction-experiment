## D-001 — Term compressed from 14 weeks to 13
**Date:** 2026-10-29
**Decision:** The proposal's 14-week plan (Table 14) is executed in the 13 active
weeks of the CISC 699 term (26 Oct 2026 – 14 Feb 2027, two scheduled breaks
excluded). The pilot and the Week 6 presentation now share a week; everything
after it shifts one week earlier.
**Reason:** The term has 13 instructional weeks, not 14.
**Affects:** M1–M8 week numbers; the two-week re-judgment gap (preserved:
human evaluation Week 9, re-judgment Week 11 = 14 calendar days).
**Status:** Closed. Decided by the researcher; no external acknowledgement
sought. Nothing in the proposal's design was dropped — only the calendar
changed — so the compression is reported as a schedule change in the final
report, not as a scope change.

## D-002 — Application stack: Node/Express + plain HTML/JS
**Date:** 2026-11-08
**Decision:** The task-tracking application (V0–V3) is a small Node/Express
server with a plain HTML/JavaScript front end and server-side sessions. No
front-end framework.
**Reason:** The proposal (§3.12) fixes the application's *behaviour* — five use
cases, user accounts, access control — but not its stack. A small codebase was
chosen because: V0 must be generated by the implementation model from the
requirements document in one pass on a free API tier, so fewer files means
fewer failed or partial generations; three copies must be made and each seeded
defect confirmed by hand (T16, T17), which scales with code size; and access
control needs real server-side sessions, which a front-end-only app cannot
provide honestly.
**Alternatives considered:** Angular + Express API (3–4× the generated code,
higher free-tier token cost, no benefit to the research question);
Flask + Jinja (equivalent, but mixes Python into the application and blurs the
harness/application separation).
**Affects:** T05 requirements document; T06 V0 generation; T16 defect seeding.
**Cannot change after:** V0 is tagged (M2, Week 3), because V1–V3 are copies.

## D-003 — Both test baselines are browser-level Playwright tests
**Date:** 2026-11-08
**Decision:** Suite A (written by the implementation model alongside V0) and
suite B (hand-written, about 20 tests) are both browser-level Playwright tests
run through pytest. The requirements document states this so that suite A comes
back in that form.
**Reason:** EQ4 compares defect detection between the two suites and the
scenario evaluation. If suite A were unit tests and suite B browser tests, the
two baselines would differ in kind and the comparison would not be clean. Making
both browser-level also keeps the baselines independent of the application's
language.
**Affects:** T05 requirements document; T07 suite B; T08 and T24 execution; EQ4.

## D-004 — Success thresholds, fixed before any data exists
**Date:** 2026-11-08
**Decision:** The proposal (§4.8) left the thresholds marked as proposed. They
are now set, by me, and frozen. They are recorded here before V0 exists, before
any scenario is written, and before any judgment is collected. The dated commit
and the `prereg-w01` tag are the evidence of that ordering.

**EQ2 — agreement.** Three figures are reported together, each with a 95%
interval:

| Symbol | What it is |
|--------|------------|
| κ_LH | LLM judge vs my first human judgments |
| κ_HH | my first judgments vs my delayed re-judgments (T27) — my own repeatability |
| — | the Landis & Koch (1977) band, for orientation only |

Pre-specified rule: **agreement is acceptable if the 95% interval for κ_LH
reaches κ_HH**, that is, the judge is not demonstrably less consistent with me
than I am with myself. Whether κ_LH ≥ 0.61 is reported alongside, with its
interval, as a conventional reference point — not as a pass or fail.

**Reason:** κ_HH is the honest ceiling. An LLM judge cannot sensibly be asked to
agree with me more than I agree with myself, and the proposal (§4.6) already
names this as the realistic target. A fixed cut at 0.61 would also be unsound
here: with about 25 human-judged runs the interval on kappa is roughly ±0.2 to
±0.3, so a point estimate landing either side of 0.61 would not support a
conclusion. The confusion matrix is reported next to kappa in every case, as
§4.6 requires.

**Affects:** T26, T27, T28; EQ2; the acceptance criterion in C05.

## D-005 — Critical-failure gate: a flag beside the scores, never replacing them
**Date:** 2026-11-08
**Decision, two parts.**

*(a) Scoring a version.* All four schemes are computed and reported side by
side for every version: binary, linear 4-3-2-1, geometric 8-4-2-1, and the
critical gate as a **separate boolean flag**. The gate does not overwrite the
weighted totals. The release-style reading is gate first; the analysis keeps
every number visible.

*(b) Judging the judge.* Critical-severity false acceptances are reported as a
**count with a Wilson interval**, not as a zero-tolerance pass or fail. If any
occurs it is reported prominently as a finding whatever the other results say.

To make (b) mean anything, the human subset (T26, about 25 runs) must contain
**at least 8 critical-severity runs, at least 4 of them known-failing** from the
defect register. This is a pre-specified sampling constraint on T26.

**Reason:** (a) EQ1 asks whether severity weighting changes conclusions versus
binary scoring. If the gate overwrote the scores, that comparison could not be
observed at all — the gate would answer EQ1 by erasing it. (b) "No critical
false acceptance" sounds strong but is close to empty at this sample size:
0 out of 4 critical runs has an upper bound near 0.6, which supports no claim
about how often the judge does this. Fixing a floor of 8 critical runs at least
makes the interval reportable and states its width honestly.

**Affects:** T20 scoring engine; T26 sampling; T28, T29; EQ1, EQ2, EQ3.

## D-006 — Thresholds set by a single researcher, declared as a limitation
**Date:** 2026-11-08
**Decision:** D-004 and D-005 were set by me alone, without external review.
This is recorded as a limitation in the final report's threats section, with the
mitigation stated plainly: the thresholds were fixed and committed before any
data existed, and the commit date and tag can be checked.
**Reason:** Setting one's own criteria is defensible when they are
pre-specified and unchangeable afterwards; it is not defensible if they are
chosen once the results are visible. The dated commit is what separates the two,
so the report states it rather than leaving the reader to wonder.
**Affects:** Report threats to validity; C06.

## D-007 — Model selection: Claude implements, Gemini judges
**Date:** 2026-11-08
**Decision:** Two models, as the proposal specifies (§4.12: "one judge model").

| Role | Model | Family | Calls |
|------|-------|--------|-------|
| Implementation | Claude (Anthropic) | Anthropic | a few: V0 and suite A, Week 3 |
| Judge | Gemini (Google), free tier | Google | about 540, Weeks 7–8 |
| Human reference | Xuan Zhao | — | about 25 blind, plus about 10 re-judged |

**Reason:** The families differ, which is the constraint §2.x imposes to avoid
self-enhancement bias — no model judges software it wrote. The judge carries
almost all of the call volume, so it is the role that needs an API with a
workable daily quota; the implementation model is called only a handful of
times and can be driven through a chat interface. A second judge model is
WP11, optional and after M6, so it is out of scope.

**To record before Week 7** (T02, still open as O5): the exact Gemini model id,
its version string as returned by the API, and the free-tier requests-per-day
limit read from the console with the date it was read.

**Reproducibility note:** the implementation model is reached through a chat
interface, so the serving model version is not exposed as precisely as an API
call would give. LLM generation is not bit-reproducible in any case. What is
reproducible, and what is therefore retained, is the exact prompt, the exact
raw output, and the tagged V0. Stated plainly in the report rather than
claimed away.

**Selection criterion for the judge — mechanical reliability only.** Before
Week 7, run a ten-call smoke test: valid JSON every time, rubric fields and
permitted values respected, stable under repetition, acceptable latency and
timeout rate. **The judge is never selected, tuned, or swapped on the basis of
how well it agrees with my own judgments.** Doing so would choose a judge that
flatters the human reference and would void EQ2. If the judge has to be
replaced after the smoke test, the reason is recorded here and reported.

**Affects:** T02, T18, T25; EQ2, EQ3; Week 7–8 pacing.

## D-008 — The requirements document mandates stable test selectors
**Date:** 2026-11-08
**Decision:** `app/REQUIREMENTS.md` §3 requires every interactive element to
carry a named `data-testid` attribute, and every message to appear in one
container with a known id.
**Reason:** The proposal (§4.12) names scenario scripting as the riskiest part
of the project and schedules it early for that reason. Unstable or invented
selectors would put Weeks 4–5 at risk and would add nothing to any research
question.
**Threat, to be reported:** this makes the generated application more testable
than an AI-generated application would typically be, which may flatter both
test suites and the scenario runner. Mitigation: the constraint is identical
for V0 and V1–V3, so it favours no version; and it says nothing about what will
be tested, so it does not leak the holdout.
**Affects:** T05, T06, T13, T15; EQ4, EQ5; report threats section.

## D-009 — Evidence is a compact trace, not raw HTML
**Date:** 2026-11-08
**Decision:** `evidence.py` writes a condensed, judge-ready trace per run:
the scenario steps, the action taken at each step, the visible text that
changed, any message shown, the final state, and the run outcome. Raw page
source is not written into the evidence passed to the judge.
**Reason:** Judge cost is dominated by evidence length. One raw HTML page runs
to tens of thousands of tokens; across 540 calls that is millions of tokens and
would not fit a free tier. Beyond cost, a long dump dilutes the judge's
attention, and the human evaluator reads the same evidence — 25 runs of raw
HTML is not readable, and an unreadable reference is a worse reference.
**Both judges see exactly the same evidence.** If they saw different material,
EQ2 would be comparing two different tasks.
**Affects:** T14, T18, T19, T25, T26; EQ2, EQ5; judge token cost.
